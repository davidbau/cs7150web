Summary

In this course we will learn the principles and practice of deep learning methods and research.

We will cover the capabilities of deep networks, the main methods for training them effectively, and the common architectures and techniques for using a deep network to process and produce images and natural language text.  In addition to getting experience using these methods, we will discuss seminal research papers, overview some of the main questions and debates that have emerged in deep learning research, and explore some current research topics.  There will be a final project where you work with a partner to choose, investigate, and write a blog exploration of a deep learning research topic centered on a paper you choose.

Homework: 5 points.  Programming and calculation exercises.

There are five homeworks, jupyter notebooks to be worked through by students individually.  In this age of LLM assistance, we will not be scoring your homework code in detail.  Instead, you earn 1 participation point for handing in your completed homework code, for a total of 5 points.  The dates in the schedule below are when each homework is meant to be done, because that is what the quizzes are timed against, but the point itself is earned whenever you hand the work in.

Test grade: 80 points.  Your understanding of the technical material is measured by two things: five short homework quizzes taken in class, and the midterm exam.

There is one homework quiz per homework assignment, given in class at the next class meeting after each homework deadline (the dates are in the schedule below).  Each quiz tests your knowledge of the methods and concepts in that homework.  The quizzes are designed to be much easier for you if you have actually worked through and understood the homework yourself, so the best preparation for a quiz is to do the homework without leaning on an LLM to do the thinking for you.

The midterm is a written, closed-book exam about foundational methods.

The two are combined into a single test grade as follows.  Let mt be your midterm score as a fraction of the maximum, and let hwq be half of your total homework quiz score as a fraction of the maximum, so that acing every quiz gives hwq = 0.5.  Then:

test grade = hwq + (1 − hwq) × mt

In other words, the homework quizzes are a way of earning up to half of the test grade — 40 of its 80 points — for free.  Equivalently, (1 − test grade) = (1 − hwq) × (1 − mt): you only lose credit where you missed on both the quizzes and the midterm.

This means the two directions both work.  If you miss a homework quiz, or several, you can still make up all of that credit on the midterm — a perfect midterm gives a perfect test grade no matter what your quiz scores were.  And if you do well on the quizzes all semester, you walk into the midterm with a large head start.

Quizzes are taken in person, in class on their scheduled day.  There is no remote or online version of a quiz.

If you miss a quiz, come to an in-person make-up session within 14 days — office hours, or a time arranged with a TA.  This is deliberately informal: it is a short conversation about that homework rather than a second sitting of the quiz, and if it is useful we will simply talk through the parts you found hard.

A make-up does not earn the quiz's points.  What it does is take the missed quiz out of your quiz average instead of scoring it as a zero, so that it neither helps nor hurts you: your hwq is then computed over the quizzes you actually took.  For example, a student who aces four quizzes and misses the fifth has hwq = 0.4 if the missed one counts as zero, and hwq = 0.5 if it is taken out.  With a 70% midterm that is the difference between 65.6 and 68 points out of 80; with a 50% midterm it is 56 against 60.  Taking the make-up can never lower your grade, and can never raise it above what you would have had by sitting every quiz.  Miss the 14-day window and the quiz counts as a zero.

If you know in advance that you will miss a quiz, tell us before the date rather than after.  And note that even a zero is not a disaster: by the formula above it simply means more of your test grade rests on the midterm, and a perfect midterm still earns a perfect test grade.

Final Project: 40 points.  A blog/webpage report and presentation about a research paper that you choose. Done by groups of 2 students (3 with permission).  Similar to the paper-reading roleplaying exercise, except that you choose the papers, you play all the roles, and instead of putting together slides, you will assemble an illustrated blog with all your analysis.  Optionally, your project may include a programming demonstration or a research extension of the concepts in the paper that you choose.

 

Late homework, and why you should not hand it in late anyway

There is no late penalty on homework and there are no late days to keep track of.  Hand in a homework whenever it is done, any time up to the last day of the semester, and you earn its point.

The catch is that the quizzes do not move.  Each homework quiz is given in class on its scheduled day whether or not you have finished the homework.  Since the quizzes are worth 80 points and the homework points are worth 5, the reason to do a homework on time is not the point — it is that the quiz a few days later is easy if you have done the work yourself and hard if you have not.  Falling behind on the homework costs you on the test grade, not on the homework grade.

 

Collaboration Policy

Collaboration is allowed, but you should think about the problems yourself before discussing them with others.  When you do seek out help, we strongly advise you to find a fellow classmate to talk with and work together rather than copying an answer. You will all learn much more by thinking collaboratively and explaining ideas to one another.  When you collaborate, you must acknowledge your collaborators by listing them explicitly.
